How it works

From photo report to settlement schedule

The capture work you already do stays exactly as it is. ClaimSight starts where the evening of Googling used to begin.

1 · Upload what you have

Drop in the inventory spreadsheet and the photo report PDF. Photographs pair to inventory lines automatically by their reference numbers — no manual matching.

2 · Research runs itself

Each item is identified from its description and photographs, the current Australian equivalent is found and priced, and discontinued models are substituted with the nearest current product — with the substitution noted.

3 · Review, then publish

Every line arrives with a confidence rating so you know where to spend your attention. Adjust what needs adjusting, then publish the schedule to a link or print it to A4.

Questions

Contents assessment, answered

How is contents depreciation calculated on an insurance claim?

Depreciation runs over the item's assessed effective life, adjusted for its condition before the loss, and stops at a residual floor rather than reaching zero. ClaimSight applies straight-line depreciation with a 10% residual floor and prints the estimated age, effective life and rate applied against every line, so a claims officer can check the working rather than take the number on trust.

What is the difference between replacement new for old and indemnity value?

Replacement new for old settles at what a new equivalent item costs today. Indemnity settles at that figure less depreciation for age and condition. ClaimSight calculates both on every claim, so the schedule can be produced on whichever basis the policy responds on without redoing the assessment.

Does this replace my field capture app?

No, and it is not trying to. Capture tools are built for the field and they are good at it. ClaimSight reads the inventory and photo reports they produce and does the valuation work afterwards. If you already use one, keep using it.

Can I send a report to an insurer without them creating an account?

Yes. Publishing a schedule produces a link that opens in any browser, optionally behind a passcode and an expiry date. The link serves a frozen copy, so adjusting a figure afterwards never changes the document already sitting on someone's claim file. You can see when it was opened.

Is the AI making up the prices?

No figure is presented without the sources it came from, and every line carries a confidence rating so you know where to look first. The assessor reviews and signs off; the software does the searching. Where an item cannot be identified confidently, the report says so rather than guessing.
